Distilling Kumo-Tabular into HistGradientBoosting quantile models

Electricity load forecasting benchmark of probabl-ai/skore-example-electricity-load (walk-forward CV with forecast weather, 8 folds of 13 weeks, horizons 1h / 12h / 24h). The question: can a family of scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingRegressor models, distilled from Kumo-Tabular with the CutMix recipe of arXiv:2610.01435, close the gap between hgb_quantile_q7 and kumo_tabular? And does post-hoc calibration help on top?

Conclusion

Distillation does not close the gap. The paper recipe (squared-error students on the teacher's 7 quantiles, CutMix at a 2:1 synthetic-to-real ratio, no ground-truth labels) moves average MAE from 859 MW to 847 MW, against 686 MW for the teacher. Two properties of this forecasting setup break the recipe: the teacher memorizes the training rows it sees as context, and CutMix of two random hours falls off the data manifold. Replacing the memorized targets with blocked out-of-fold teacher predictions fixes the collapsed intervals and makes the median worse.

The gain that does show up comes from the features, with no teacher involved. Differences of the load lags, and a residual target relative to the last observed load, bring a label-trained HGB to 747 MW. Leaving the median alone and shifting the other quantiles from the previous 4 folds then puts its 90% coverage at 0.91 and its 7-level pinball loss at 232 MW, against 213 MW for the teacher. Distilling on top of those same features is worse than training them on the labels.

One combination was stopped before it finished: out-of-fold teacher targets together with the lag-difference features (6 of 12 folds done). It is not in the tables.

Iterations

  1. Reproduction of kumo_tabular and hgb_quantile_q7 on an AWS L4 GPU.
  2. Distillation into HGB students: the paper recipe, real rows only, out-of-fold targets, lag-difference features, and the teacher diagnostics.
  3. Post-hoc calibration from the predictions of the 4 preceding walk-forward folds.
